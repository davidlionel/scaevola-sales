# Discovery

How deal support prepares the person running a sales call. Discovery follows the owner's [buyer lens](../method/buyer-lens.md): we learn the buyer's real struggling moment and what it means to them, not just their requirements. The questions below come from switch interviews in Jobs to Be Done: ask for stories, not opinions.

## The aim of a first call
Learn enough to answer four questions, then agree on one next step:
1. **What happened** that made this a problem now? (The trigger.)
2. **What does it cost** them to leave it alone? (In their words and, if they say it, in numbers.)
3. **Who decides,** and who else has to be comfortable?
4. **What would make switching feel safe?** (Their anxiety and habit.)

## Questions (pick a few; don't interrogate)
**The moment**
- "Walk me through when this first became a problem. What was going on that week?"
- "What made you take the call now, rather than three months ago?"

**What they've tried**
- "What have you tried so far? What happened?"
- "What are you using today, and what do you like about it?" (Habit: what they'd miss.)

**What it means**
- "If this were fixed, what would be different for you personally?"
- "What happens if it's still like this in six months?"

**Deciding**
- "Who else cares about this? What would they need to see?"
- "What would make this feel risky to change?"

## The four forces (score after the call)
- **Push:** how bad is the current situation?
- **Pull:** how attractive is the new one?
- **Anxiety:** what worries them about switching?
- **Habit:** what keeps them where they are?

A deal moves when push and pull beat anxiety and habit. The follow-up and proposal should lower anxiety (a small first step, clear scope, what they keep if they stop) and make the habit easy to leave.

## Notes
- Record their words as close to verbatim as possible, marked as quotes. Keep "what they said they want" apart from "what they seem to be buying".
- Write down every objection, even unspoken ones you noticed. New ones go to `offer.md` as drafts.
- End every call with a dated next step, or a clear "not now" with a date to check back.

A motion repo may add its own qualification method (for example, a structured one for sales-led deals). It adds to this page; it doesn't replace it.
